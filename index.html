Programme 1

Astronomy Course

A beginner-friendly course that takes you from "why do the stars move?" to setting up a telescope and finding galaxies on your own. No prior astronomy is needed: Class 10 maths is enough, and every calculation has a worked example.

Open toAll students and teachers

What you will learn

Unit 1

Motion of the Sky and Time-Keeping

  • Why the Sun, Moon and stars rise and set, and how to find Polaris
  • The real reason for seasons; equinoxes, solstices and Zero Shadow Day
  • Phases of the Moon, solar and lunar eclipses, Rahu and Ketu
  • The Panchang: tithi, nakshatra and why weekdays are named after planets
  • Why Makar Sankranti falls on 14 January; Hijri and Gregorian calendars, leap years and IST

Unit 2

Sky, Stars and Constellations

  • The celestial sphere; altitude–azimuth and RA–Dec coordinates
  • Constellations through history: Babylonian, Greek, Arabic, Indian and the modern 88
  • Asterisms, the zodiac and the 20 brightest stars with their Indian names
  • Star-hopping routes for every season and reading all-sky charts
  • A practical stargazing field guide for India

Unit 3

Magnitudes, Telescopes and Catalogues

  • The magnitude scale: how bright a star looks versus how bright it really is
  • Refractors, reflectors and Schmidt-Cassegrains: how telescopes work
  • Telescope maths: magnification, field of view, light grasp and limiting magnitude
  • Setting up, aligning and using a telescope yourself
  • Messier and NGC catalogues: nebulae, star clusters and galaxies

Programme 2

Star Party Near Tansa

One night under a dark sky, away from city lights, with telescopes, binoculars and a guided tour of the stars.

What you will do

  1. Sunset and set-upArrive before dark, learn the site, and set up the telescopes with us.
  2. Naked-eye sky tourA laser-guided walk across the sky: find north with Polaris, then Orion, Taurus, Gemini and the bright winter stars with their Indian names.
  3. Telescope viewsCraters and mountains on the Moon, Saturn's rings, Jupiter and its four moons, the Pleiades and the Orion Nebula.
  4. Star-hopping challengeUse the star-hopping routes from the course to find objects on your own.
  5. Sky photography basicsPhone and camera settings for photographing constellations and the Milky Way.
  6. Stories of the skyNakshatras, Greek myths and how ancient people used the stars for time-keeping, followed by questions and answers.
  7. Astronomy quizA team quiz on the night sky, planets and space, with small prizes.
  8. Night games and musicFun group games under the stars. Bring your guitar for a sing-along!
Bring: warm clothes, a water bottle, a mat or sheet, mosquito repellent, a torch covered with red paper or tape, your college ID, and your guitar or other instrument if you play one. Leave jewellery and other valuables at home.
Travel: bus from college.
Parental consent: every participant must submit the consent form signed by a parent or guardian.
